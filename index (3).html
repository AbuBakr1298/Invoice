How to Calculate Invoice Tax and Discounts Correctly

Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Every invoice total comes from four numbers: the subtotal, the discount, the tax and the total due. Getting the order wrong is the most common reason totals are off, and it usually means the client is charged tax on money they never pay. This guide shows the order that works, with a full example you can check by hand.

The four numbers on every invoice

  • Subtotal: the sum of every line total, where a line total is quantity × rate.
  • Discount: a reduction in the price, usually a percentage of the subtotal.
  • Tax: a percentage charged on the taxable amount.
  • Total: what the client actually pays.

The order that gets it right: discount first, then tax

In most cases, a discount you show on the invoice reduces the price of what you are selling, so tax is calculated on the reduced amount:

Discount       = Subtotal × discount rate
Taxable amount = Subtotal − Discount
Tax            = Taxable amount × tax rate
Total          = Taxable amount + Tax

A worked example

Three line items, a 10% discount and 8% tax:

DescriptionQtyRateAmount
Brand design10$50.00$500.00
Website development20$75.00$1,500.00
Hosting setup1$100.00$100.00
StepCalculationResult
Subtotal500.00 + 1,500.00 + 100.00$2,100.00
Discount (10%)2,100.00 × 10%−$210.00
Taxable amount2,100.00 − 210.00$1,890.00
Tax (8%)1,890.00 × 8%$151.20
Total due1,890.00 + 151.20$2,041.20

Now compare the wrong order. If tax is calculated on the full $2,100.00 before the discount, tax is $168.00 and the total becomes $2,100.00 − $210.00 + $168.00 = $2,058.00. That is $16.80 too much: the client has paid 8% tax on the $210.00 discount.

Percentage or flat-amount discount?

If you want to give a flat amount off, convert it to a percentage of the subtotal: divide the discount by the subtotal. A $100 discount on a $2,000 subtotal is 100 ÷ 2,000 = 5%.

If your prices already include tax

Some price lists include tax. To find the price before tax, divide by one plus the tax rate: a price of 118 that includes 18% tax is 118 ÷ 1.18 = 100, so the tax is 18. QuickInvoice adds tax on top of the prices you enter, so enter the pre-tax prices.

One tax rate per invoice

QuickInvoice applies a single tax rate to the whole invoice. If different items on one invoice carry different rates, for example a standard and a reduced rate, split them into separate invoices or use accounting software that supports tax per line.

Rounding

Round each line total to the cent, then add the rounded lines, then round the discount and the tax to the cent. That way the figures printed on the invoice always add up. Some jurisdictions specify where rounding must happen, so follow the rule that applies to you.

When the rules differ

Tax treatment can vary by country and by type of discount. A rebate granted after the sale, or a discount funded by a third party, may be treated differently from a discount shown on the invoice. If you are unsure, ask an accountant or your tax authority. This guide is general information, not tax advice.
