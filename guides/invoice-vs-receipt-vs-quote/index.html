Invoice vs Receipt vs Quote: What Is the Difference?

Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Quotes, invoices and receipts are easy to mix up, and sending the wrong one can confuse your client or delay payment. In short: a quote offers a price before work starts, an invoice asks for payment after the work, and a receipt confirms the money arrived.

This guide shows what each document is for, what it should contain and how they fit together. When you are ready to bill, you can create an invoice for free.

The short answer

DocumentSentPurposeTypically includes
QuoteBefore work startsOffers a price for defined workScope, price, validity period, terms
InvoiceAfter delivery or at a milestoneRequests paymentInvoice number, dates, line items, total due, payment details
ReceiptAfter paymentConfirms payment was receivedAmount paid, date, method, reference to the invoice

Quote (or quotation)

A quote is your offer to do specific work for a specific price. Send one when a client asks what a project will cost. Include what is covered, what is not, the price, how long the offer is valid and the payment terms you propose. A quote usually states a price you are prepared to stand behind for a set period, and once the client accepts it, it forms the basis of your agreement.

Quote or estimate? An estimate is an approximation that can change as the work becomes clearer; a quote is a firm offer. The words are used differently by country and industry, so say clearly on the document which one you mean.

Invoice

An invoice is a request for payment for work done or goods delivered. It starts the payment clock: the due date follows from your terms. It should carry a unique number, both parties’ details, itemised charges, the total due and how to pay. Our guide to creating a freelance invoice covers each field, and the one on payment terms helps you pick the due date.

Receipt

A receipt confirms that a payment was received. It states the amount paid, the date, the payment method and which invoice it settles. Many freelancers simply mark the invoice as paid or send a short payment confirmation, but some clients, and some tax rules, require a formal receipt, particularly for cash payments. Check what applies to you.

How they work together

  1. The client asks about a project and you send a quote.
  2. The client accepts it, by signing, sending a purchase order or approving it by email.
  3. You deliver the work.
  4. You send an invoice for the agreed amount.
  5. The client pays.
  6. You send a receipt or payment confirmation.

Other documents you will come across

  • Purchase order (PO): issued by the buyer to authorise a purchase. Put the PO number on your invoice.
  • Proforma invoice: a preliminary invoice sent before delivery, sometimes used for advance payment or customs. It is not usually the final invoice.
  • Credit note: reduces or cancels an earlier invoice, for example to correct a mistake or issue a refund.

Common mix-ups to avoid

  • Sending an invoice before the price was agreed in a quote or contract.
  • Using the same number sequence for quotes and invoices, which makes your records hard to follow.
  • Labelling a quote as an invoice, so the client believes payment is already due.
