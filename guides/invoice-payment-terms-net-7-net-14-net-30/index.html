Invoice Payment Terms: Net 7, Net 14, Net 30 Explained

Updated October 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Payment terms tell your client when an invoice must be paid. Clear terms set expectations, give you a deadline to follow up against, and protect your cash flow. This guide explains the common terms, shows how to choose one, and gives wording you can adapt for your own invoices.

What “Net 30” means

“Net 30” means the full amount is due 30 days after the invoice date. “Net 14” means 14 days and “Net 7” means 7 days. An invoice dated 1 November on Net 14 terms is due on 15 November; on Net 30 terms it is due on 1 December.

Some clients count from the day they receive the invoice rather than the date printed on it. To avoid any doubt, write both the term and the actual due date on the invoice.

Common payment terms at a glance

TermMeaningTypical use
Due on receiptPayment requested immediatelySmall jobs, first-time clients, deposits
Net 7Due 7 days after the invoice dateShort projects and quick-turnaround work
Net 14Due 14 days after the invoice dateCommon for freelancers and small agencies
Net 30Due 30 days after the invoice dateBusinesses with monthly payment runs
Net 45 / 60 / 90Due 45, 60 or 90 days after the invoice dateLarge organisations; weigh the longer wait against the value of the contract

Early-payment discounts: how “2/10 Net 30” works

“2/10 Net 30” means the client may deduct 2% if they pay within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 30 days. On a $1,000 invoice, that is $980 if paid within 10 days and $1,000 by day 30.

QuickInvoice shows one due date, so put the early-payment wording in the Notes & Terms field. Offer a discount only if getting paid sooner is worth the reduction to you.

How to choose your terms

  • Your cash flow: shorter terms bring money in sooner, which matters most for freelancers with few clients.
  • Client size and habits: larger companies often pay on a fixed cycle and may expect 30 days or more.
  • Project size and risk: for bigger projects, ask for a deposit and invoice in milestones instead of one large invoice at the end.
  • Local rules: some countries regulate commercial payment periods and interest on late payments (the EU’s Late Payment Directive is one example), so check the rules that apply to you and your client.

Whatever you choose, agree it in the contract before the work starts, not for the first time on the invoice. Our freelance invoice guide shows where terms sit on the page.

Deposits and milestone invoices

For larger projects, ask for a deposit before work begins and invoice the rest at agreed milestones. This reduces your risk, keeps cash coming in during a long project and makes each invoice smaller and easier to approve. Write the schedule into your contract.

How to word late-payment terms

Keep the wording short, specific and polite. Two examples you can adapt:

  • “Payment is due within 14 days of the invoice date.”
  • “Late payments may incur interest at the rate stated in our agreement.”

A late fee or interest generally has to be agreed in advance, and the maximum you can charge varies by country. Mention it on the invoice only if it is already part of your contract.

A reminder routine that keeps relationships friendly

  1. A few days before the due date: a short, friendly reminder with the invoice attached.
  2. On the due date: confirm the invoice is scheduled for payment.
  3. After the due date: a polite but firm follow-up that names the invoice number, the amount and the original due date.

Keep copies of every message. If an invoice stays unpaid, that record is the first thing you will need.
